The 2026 AnVIL Community Conference (ACC2026) brought together researchers, developers, educators, data scientists, and community members to share new work, explore emerging directions in genomic data science, and discuss how AnVIL can continue to support an evolving research community. Held at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard in Cambridge, Massachusetts, the meeting combined hands-on workshops and community discussion on August 31 with a full day of scientific talks, posters, and networking on September 1.

ACC2026 received 117 registrations for in-person participation and 168 registrations for virtual participation. The meeting was primarily an in-person gathering, with concurrent virtual attendance remaining around 25 participants.

## Workshops and Community Discussion

ACC2026 opened with an afternoon of concurrent workshops and discussion designed to support both new and experienced AnVIL users. Sessions included AnVIL101, an AnVIL program retrospective, the All of Us and AnVIL Imputation Service workshop, a discussion of AI-enabled analyses on AnVIL, a DREAM Challenge session, and a closing Fishbowl discussion.

### AnVIL101 and Platform Retrospective

The first workshop block paired an introduction to the AnVIL platform with a retrospective discussion about the program. AnVIL101, led by Ava Hoffman, provided an entry point for participants who were newer to the platform. In parallel, Jonathan Lawson led a retrospective focused on what has worked well across the AnVIL program, where challenges remain, and what could be improved.

### Applications on AnVIL

The second workshop block focused on scientific and technical applications. Chris Kachulis led a session on the All of Us and AnVIL Imputation Service, Vincent Carey led a discussion exploring AI-enabled analyses with AnVIL, and Kyle Ellrott led a DREAM Challenge session.

The AI-enabled analyses discussion explored both current opportunities and important limitations for using generative AI tools in cloud-based genomic analysis. Participants discussed ways AI could help researchers navigate complex datasets, troubleshoot software, generate documentation, work across analysis environments, and lower technical barriers for users who may not be experts in AnVIL or data science.

The discussion also emphasized the need to approach AI use thoughtfully when controlled-access data and credentials are involved. Participants considered ways to experiment with AI without exposing protected data, including working in environments that are not tied to controlled-access credentials, while also discussing what responsible, more integrated AI support might look like in the future.

### Fishbowl Discussion: Data, Tools, and Mission

Day 1 concluded with a Fishbowl discussion led by Frederick Tan and Jeremy Goecks. The format invited participants to move between observing and actively joining the conversation, with discussion organized around three broad questions: what makes data valuable, what new tools or AI capabilities would be useful on AnVIL, and what larger scientific challenges the AnVIL community could tackle collectively.

<Figure
  alt="ACC2026 Fishbowl discussion focused on data, tools, and community-wide scientific challenges."
  src="/consortia/news/acc2026-fishbowl.webp"
/>

Several recurring themes emerged from the discussion:

- **Making data easier to discover and use.** Participants emphasized the importance of robust metadata, clearer descriptions of how datasets can be used, and research use cases that help users understand the scientific value of available data.
- **Supporting cross-dataset analysis.** The group discussed how AnVIL could make it easier to combine datasets and metadata across resources and institutions, while recognizing that different researchers want different levels of upstream harmonization.
- **Using AI to lower technical barriers.** Potential applications included assisting with configuration, authentication, software troubleshooting, documentation, dataset interrogation, and common platform pain points.
- **Creating incentives for data sharing.** Participants noted that data sharing is not rewarded as consistently as publication and discussed the need for stronger incentives, recognition, and citation practices.
- **Building community-wide scientific challenges.** The group explored whether challenge-based approaches, cloud credits, and other incentives could motivate collaborative analyses and encourage broader participation across the AnVIL community.

A central takeaway from the afternoon was that AI can already support useful work around documentation, software, and open or non-protected information, while broader integration with controlled-access research environments would require careful technical and policy consideration.

## Summary

ACC2026 brought the AnVIL community together to share research, demonstrate new tools and resources, and discuss where genomic data science in the cloud is headed next. Across workshops, keynotes, invited and submitted talks, posters, and community discussion, several themes appeared repeatedly: making data easier to find and use, improving interoperability across resources, reducing technical barriers to analysis, exploring responsible applications of AI, and continuing to build a community around shared genomic data and infrastructure.

The meeting also reinforced the importance of creating opportunities for users and developers to shape the platform together. Discussions throughout ACC2026 surfaced both practical challenges and ambitious ideas, from better metadata and easier cross-dataset analysis to AI-assisted workflows and community-wide scientific challenges.

Thank you to everyone who presented, facilitated workshops, shared a poster, participated in discussions, joined virtually, or helped make ACC2026 possible. Recordings from the conference are available in the [ACC2026 YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLf-1VFMcx5CA).
