Genomic Answers for Kids (GA4K) has released new data to help experts find breakthrough answers and treatments for rare, pediatric genetic conditions. This Version 6 release expands the number of available sequences and analyses by more than 15%.

The v6 release on the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) AnVIL platform contains the most complete raw sequencing data available from GA4K. Researchers looking for read-level data at medium to medium-high depth, particularly in pediatric populations or families, can request access at the links below.

The new v6 data release includes:

- Over 2,200 long-read genome sequences
- Over 14,000 short-read genome and exome analyses
- Nearly 400 snapshots of patient transcriptomes and epigenomes in individual cells using single-cell RNA (scRNA) and sc open chromatin (scATAC)
- Over 2,700 whole-genome sequences for methylome interpretation, including bulk bisulfite and PacBio-methyl-tagged HiFi sequencing
- Nearly 250 functional assessments in available patient tissues using full length cDNA sequences by IsoSeq (PacBio) methodology

To learn more about the Children’s Mercy GA4K pediatric data repository — a study that generates genomic data and collects phenotypic and family history information to help thousands of kids and their loved ones — visit [childrensmercy.org](https://www.childrensmercy.org/).

The GA4K repository offers an unusually large dataset for rare diseases. Highlights from the v6 release include more long-read data, newly-described phenotypes associated with rare disease profiles, and sequencing from recently-enrolled individuals. The GA4K repository has a substantial proportion of parent-offspring trios, and common clinical diagnoses represented are global developmental delay, seizures, autism, and ADHD.

Data types and processing methods include PacBio (Cell Ranger and Deep Variant) for long-read sequencing, Illumina (GSA and DRAGEN) and MGI for short-read and whole-genome sequencing, Illumina for exome sequencing, and 10x single-cell analysis.

Access requests for this controlled data are available through dbGaP under accession [phs002206.v6.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs002206.v6.p1). The data is available at https://explore.anvilproject.org/datasets or https://duos.org/datalibrary/anvil.

Variants identified in Genomic Answers for Kids are also deposited in the open-access [Beacon Network](https://beacon-network.org/#/).

Children’s Mercy is a nationally recognized and leading pediatric health system in Kansas City. Genomic Answers for Kids (GA4K), a research initiative of the Children’s Mercy Research Institute, is a pediatric data repository to facilitate the search for answers and novel treatments for pediatric genetic conditions. The study generates genomic data and collects health information, including phenotypic and family history information, for thousands of children and their families. Controlled-access data is available for the study of pediatric disease.
