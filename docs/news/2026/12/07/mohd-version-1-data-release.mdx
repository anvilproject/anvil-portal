The Multi-Omics for Health and Disease (MOHD) Consortium, a National Institutes of Health (NIH) initiative begun in 2023, has released its first dataset from 956 participants, more than half the expected final cohort. With the goal of enabling new discoveries about how health and disease develop, progress, and change over time, MOHD has generated a deeply-characterized multi-omic dataset from a heterogeneous participant population, helping fill gaps in existing genomic research resources.

This Version 1 release on the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) AnVIL platform contains raw deidentified participant data including whole-genome, RNA, and chromatin sequencing data, paired with mass-spectrometry data and deep phenotypic and contextual data. Researchers looking for genomic and multi-omic data from a heterogeneous population, or who are interested in related activities such as understanding gene regulation or mining phenotypic data, can request access. All data is consented for General Research Use (GRU).

Access requests for this controlled data are available through dbGaP under [phs003765.v1.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003765.v1.p1). The data is available at https://explore.anvilproject.org/datasets or https://duos.org/datalibrary/anvil.

The v1 data release includes:

- 915 whole-genome sequencing experiments
- 915 whole-genome bisulfite sequencing experiments
- 492 ATAC-seq experiments
- 922 RNA-seq experiments
- 1,210 metabolic experiments
- 1,211 lipidomic experiments
- 690 chemical exposomic experiments
- 612 metallomic experiments
- 956 participants with phenotypic and clinical datasets

The dataset is particularly heterogeneous, reflecting genetic ancestries that are often underrepresented in large-scale genomic studies (including participants with admixed genetic backgrounds) and representing a range of geographic recruitment locations, from community-based health systems to urban medical centers. MOHD study sites specifically investigate adult and pediatric liver disease, preeclampsia, pediatric asthma, chronic kidney disease, and diabetes. Rich phenotypic data can enable researchers to explore additional conditions, such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, stroke, heart attack, cancer, coronary disease, and obesity.

The phenotypic and contextual data available on the AnVIL platform is particularly rich, covering a broad range of clinical, environmental, and participant-reported information. Datasets contain lifestyle and health behaviors, clinical and medical history (including physical measures, family and personal medical history, female reproductive history, and medications), and social and structural determinants of health.

MOHD aims to integrate genomic and other molecular measurements with clinical, environmental, behavioral, and participant-reported information across multiple disease populations, life stages, and time points. The high-quality, interoperable multi-omic datasets from deeply-phenotyped participants available in this and future releases will illuminate health changes over time.

To learn more about the MOHD v1 data release, [watch a talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=enlrFxqkGYg&t=1852s) at the AnVIL Community Conference 2026 by Jill Moore (University of Massachusetts), one of the MOHD Principal Investigators, and read a [Q&A](/news/2026/12/07/mohd-jill-moore-qa) with Dr. Moore.

Individual-level non-sequence molecular data and summary-level phenotypic and contextual data is also deposited on the open-access MOHD Data Portal at [data.mohdconsortium.org](https://data.mohdconsortium.org). Information about study design, protocols, and phenotypic and contextual domains is available at [mohdconsortium.org](https://mohdconsortium.org).

Participants will be surveyed at several timepoints that vary depending on the condition studied. Future data releases from MOHD will reflect these snapshots in time, and ultimately include a planned cohort of 1,800 participants. The next data release is slated for the second quarter of 2027.

With contributions from NHGRI, the National Cancer Institute (NCI), and the National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences (NIEHS), the Multi-Omics for Health and Disease Consortium is a collaborative initiative that will advance the application of multi-omic technologies to study health and disease in ancestrally diverse populations. Six disease-study sites recruit participants: Border Health Research Cohort (BHRC-CCHC) in Brownsville, Texas, affiliated with UT Health and Vanderbilt University Medical Center (VUMC); Exposomic Profiling in Airway disease to uNravel Determinants of Asthma (EXPAND-Asthma) in San Francisco, California, affiliated with UC San Francisco; Multi-Omics for Maternal Health (MOM-Health) in San Diego, CA, and Nashville, Tennessee, affiliated with UC San Diego and VUMC; Columbia University Chronic Kidney Disease (Columbia-CKD) in New York, NY; Longitudinal integration of environmental exposures, omics, and childhood NAFLD (LEON) in Los Angeles, CA, affiliated with the University of Southern California; and University of Illinois Chicago Diabetes and Kidney Disease (UIC-DKD) in Chicago, IL, affiliated with University of Illinois Chicago and Boston University. Molecular data is produced at the Omics Production Center (OPC) at Washington University St. Louis, and processed by the Data Analysis and Coordination Center (DACC) affiliated with UMass Chan Medical School, Stanford University, and the University of Washington.
