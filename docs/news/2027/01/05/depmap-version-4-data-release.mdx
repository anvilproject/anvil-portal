The Broad Institute's Cancer Dependency Map (DepMap) project has released an expanded dataset of whole-genome-sequencing and RNA-sequencing data for cancer cell models. The Version 4 release on the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) AnVIL platform contains 375 additional cell lines and three new disease-specific consent groups, in addition to the previous two. Researchers looking to use this data for a wide range of cancer research applications can request access at the links below.

This v4 release provides the underlying data for 189 new NextGen cancer models profiled in the recently published Neiswender JV, Maffa S, Brenan L, et al. [A dependency map enhanced with next-generation 3D cancer models](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10843-7). Nature. 2026;657. doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10843-7. The team used advances in NextGen 3D cancer models to enhance DepMap, covering new genomic and molecular subtypes and identifying new biomarker-associated vulnerabilities. The study reported on 10 cancer types: ampullary, biliary tract, brain, breast, colorectal, esophageal-stomach, ovarian, pancreatic, prostate, and uterine.

The new v4 data release includes:

- 50X whole-exome sequencing (WES)
- 30X whole-genome sequencing (WGS)
- 100X RNA sequencing
- Associated model characteristics (metadata) as available on [DepMap.org](https://depmap.org)

The data release brings the total of raw sequencing data available from DepMap to nearly 900 cell lines. DepMap genomically profiles large panels of human cancer models using whole-genome sequencing, whole-exome sequencing, RNA sequencing, and other -omics modalities. The project employs genome-wide CRISPR knockout screening and drug sensitivity testing in barcoded cell lines to discover new targets and biomarkers for precision cancer therapy. The DepMap team is excited to enable more researchers to leverage this resource to drive progress in understanding cancer dependencies and developing effective treatments.

Access requests for this controlled data are available through dbGaP under accession [phs003444.v4.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003444.v4.p1). The data is available at https://explore.anvilproject.org/datasets or https://duos.org/datalibrary/anvil.

Access restrictions include Health/Medical/Biomedical Methods Development (HMB-MDS) and General Research Use (GRU). There are three new disease-specific cohorts that will require researchers to apply for access, even if they already have approval for this project: cancer (DS-CA-MDS) with 95 models, endometrial cancer (DS-ECA-MDS) with 1 model, and gastrointestinal cancer (DS-GICANCER-MDS) with 11 models.

New data will be added to the DepMap AnVIL datasets as it becomes available in the future. Processed DepMap data and analyses are free and available to be explored on the [DepMap.org](https://depmap.org) portal.

The DepMap project is committed to systematically accelerating precision cancer medicine by developing comprehensive, large-scale functional datasets that are publicly accessible to the research community. Launched in 2017 at the Broad Institute, and building off the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia ([PMID:31068700](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31068700/)) and Project Achilles, the DepMap project aimed to provide widespread insights into how specific genes influence cancer cell survival and response to treatments ([PMID:28753430](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28753430/)). The DepMap project continues to expand, integrating genomic, transcriptomic, and drug response data, and has become a vital resource for the cancer research community ([PMID:39468210](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39468210/)). DepMap’s collaborative approach encourages contributions from researchers worldwide, fostering innovation and discovery in cancer treatment strategies. To further advance this mission, the Broad established the DepMap Consortium, an industry/academic partnership dedicated to expanding DepMap's capabilities to accelerate precision cancer medicine.
