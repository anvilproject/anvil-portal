The eMERGE (electronic MEdical Records and GEnomics) network has released preliminary data from its fourth phase. This part of the project focused on creating a unified genome-informed risk report combining cross-ancestry polygenic risk, monogenic risk, family history risk, and clinical risk for 11 common conditions, including cancers and cardiovascular disease. The risk report included recommended actions, and the network is examining the uptake of care recommendations after return of results.

This Phase IV Version 1 release on the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) AnVIL platform offers the first glimpse of changes in healthcare actions six months after participants received the risk reports. New data types in the release include the results of the risk assessment, participant surveys, and family history. New genomic and electronic-health-record data is also available.

The release contains raw sequencing data, VCF files, phenotype data, and participant surveys. Researchers looking for a large dataset with rich contextual data — which is relevant to common complex diseases and reflects participant populations (including pediatric) not well-represented in existing genomic repositories — can request access at the links below.

Approximately half of the nearly 24,000 participants reflected in the genomic data are individuals not typically involved in research, specifically from groups not well-represented in currently available genome-wide association (GWAS) datasets.

The Phase IV v1 data release includes:

- 23,857 VCF files from GWAS genotyped with Illumina Global Diversity Array
- 23,857 polygenic risk scores, both raw (plink2) and adjusted after an ancestry calibration model generated by the Broad Institute
- 23,857 genome-informed risk assessment reports
- 20,428 MeTree family histories
- 19,489 clinical monogenic sequences across 16 genes by Invitae/Labcorp
- 11,989 participant surveys collected six months after return of results
- 26,502 electronic health records collected six months after return of results

By combining clinical, family history, and genomic data together, this unique dataset enables investigators to examine a more comprehensive view of patient health and care in individuals across the United States. The genome-informed risk assessment reports returned to participants cover 11 conditions: asthma, atrial fibrillation, breast cancer, chronic kidney disease, coronary heart disease, colorectal cancer, hypercholesterolemia, obesity, prostate cancer, Type 1 diabetes mellitus, and Type 2 diabetes mellitus. Related monogenic-sequenced genes include BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, PTEN, TP53, STK11, LMNA, EPCAM, MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, APOB, LDLR, LDLRAP1, and PCSK9. The GWAS data was derived from the Global Diversity Array at the Broad Institute. The majority of the de-identified participant data is from adults (~20,000), with some pediatric data.

Access requests for this controlled data are available through dbGaP under accession [phs003905.v1.p1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs003905.v1.p1). The data is available at https://explore.anvilproject.org/datasets or https://duos.org/datalibrary/anvil.

The data is described in three recent papers:

- Phase IV v1 release data: Lawson LP, Prows CA, Cortopassi J, et al. Am J Hum Genet. 2026;113(4). doi:10.1016/j.ajhg.2026.02.016
- Development of genetic data platform and polygenic risk assessments: Lennon NJ, Kottyan LC, Kachulis C, et al. [Selection, optimization and validation of ten chronic disease polygenic risk scores for clinical implementation in diverse US populations](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-02796-z). Nat Med. 2024;30. doi:10.1038/s41591-024-02796-z
- Study design and genome informed risk assessments: Linder JE, Allworth A, Bland HT, et al. [Returning integrated genomic risk and clinical recommendations: The eMERGE study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1098360023000023). Genet Med. 2023;25(4). doi:10.1016/j.gim.2023.100006

The next data release (Phase IV Version 2) from eMERGE is expected in late spring 2027, and will be a more robust dataset collated 12 months after the return of study results to participants.

The primary goal of the eMERGE Network is to develop, disseminate, and apply research approaches that combine biorepositories with electronic health record (EHR) systems for genomic discovery and genomic medicine implementation research. The eMERGE network is a National Institutes of Health (NIH)-organized and funded consortium through the NHGRI and U.S. medical research institutions. Study sites for this data release were the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, the Cincinnati Children’s Hospital Medical Center, Columbia University, the Icahn School of Medicine at Mt. Sinai, Mass General Brigham, the Mayo Clinic, Northwestern University, the University of Alabama at Birmingham, the University of Washington, and the Vanderbilt University Medical Center (VUMC). VUMC also led the eMERGE Network Coordinating Center, and worked in partnership with the Broad Institute and Duke University on this data release. To learn more, visit [emerge-network.org](https://emerge-network.org/).
